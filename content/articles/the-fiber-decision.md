Added fiber was in this formula. It came out on 14 February, and the change log records that in
a single line. This is the longer version, because it is the decision we were least comfortable
with and the one most likely to be read as cost-cutting.

It was not cost-cutting. Fiber is one of the cheapest things we could have put in the scoop.

## Why we wanted it

Fiber is the rare ingredient with a long, unglamorous and genuinely large body of research
behind it, and people ask for it by name. Unlike most ingredient requests we get, that one is
reasonable. It would also have made the label look more complete, which we are including in this
list because it was honestly part of the appeal.

## An honest dose does not fit the scoop

A serving of Crave Balance is one 5 g scoop, and about 3.3 g of that is already committed:
3,000 mg of myo-inositol, 200 mg of L-theanine, 75 mg of D-chiro-inositol, 28 mg of saffron
extract and 200 mcg of chromium picolinate. What remains carries the flavour, the acid, the
sweetener, and the small amount of room a powder needs in order to dissolve rather than clump.

The fiber doses used in the research are measured in grams — several of them a day, not a
fraction of one. Carrying a dose worth printing would have meant doubling the scoop or removing
something. There was nothing in there we were willing to remove, and a scoop you have to take
twice is the capsule problem in a different container.

## The texture

The viscous fibers — glucomannan, psyllium and their relatives — work by thickening water. That
is not a side effect to be formulated around. It is the mechanism.

The directions for this drink say to stir one scoop into 250 to 300 ml of water, and that it
clears in a few seconds. A fiber dose large enough to be worth having would replace that with a
glass you need to drink before it sets. We know how that ends, because it is the same ending as
nine capsules.

> The version of this formula with fiber in it is a better label and a worse drink.

## The cheap version, and why we did not ship it

There was an obvious compromise available. Put a gram of inulin in — small enough to leave the
texture alone — and print "with prebiotic fiber" on the front of the pack. It would not have
been a lie.

It would have been a dose chosen by the marketing requirement rather than by the research, which
is the same instinct that produces a proprietary blend, arriving by a more respectable route.
And we would have had to sit that gram next to 3,000 mg of inositol on a dose card that
publishes every milligram and inch of reasoning. The card is unforgiving that way, which is most
of its value.

We would rather have a shorter label we can defend line by line.

## What would change this

Fiber is not wrong. It is wrong here, at this serving size, in this glass of water.

If we make something where the serving size is not the binding constraint — a larger scoop
taken at a different time of day, or a separate product altogether — then the argument starts
again from the top, and we will publish that reasoning the same way we published this.

Until then the honest answer is the one the label already gives: there is no added fiber in
Crave Balance. The reasons are texture and dose. Cost was never one of them.
