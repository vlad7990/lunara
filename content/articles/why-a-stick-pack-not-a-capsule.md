Capsules would have been easier in every way that matters to us and almost none that matter to
you. They cost less to fill. They ship lighter. They need no flavour work at all. They look like
medicine, which sells. We are making a powder anyway, and the reason is arithmetic rather than
philosophy.

## The arithmetic

A size 00 capsule holds somewhere around 600 to 800 mg of a powder like inositol, depending on
how finely it is milled and how well it packs. One serving of Crave Balance carries 3,000 mg of
myo-inositol and 75 mg of D-chiro-inositol. The inositol alone is four to five capsules.

Add 200 mg of L-theanine, 28 mg of saffron extract and 200 mcg of chromium picolinate and the
honest count lands between six and nine capsules, taken every day, without gaps.

Nobody takes nine capsules a day. People take nine for a week, then six because six felt like
enough, and then the bottle moves to the back of the cupboard where it becomes something to feel
guilty about. That is not a failure of discipline. It is a format asking for more than it is
worth.

## The dose is the product

Inositol is a consistency ingredient, not an as-needed one — that sentence is in the directions
on the jar, and it is the constraint the whole format has to serve. A format that quietly
reduces the dose, by making the dose tedious, has undone the formula while leaving the label
intact.

The label would still have read 3,000 mg. That is exactly the problem.

> A dose you do not take is not a dose. It is a number on a label.

## Why the other product is capsules

Cycle Ritual is capsules — three of them, once a day. That is not us contradicting ourselves; it
is the same rule producing a different answer. Its actives fit comfortably into three capsules,
so it gets capsules. Crave Balance's do not, so it gets a scoop and a glass of water.

The format follows the dose. It has never once gone the other way round.

## What the powder costs us

The unflattering part is that a powder has to taste of something. Three grams of inositol
dissolved in water tastes faintly sweet and quite flat, so the ingredient list ends with a
flavour, an acid and a sweetener — three lines a capsule would never have needed, and three
lines someone will eventually write to us about.

We would rather defend those three lines than explain why the serving is nine capsules.

## And the jar

The sticks and the jar hold the same powder at the same serving. The jar is the everyday format
and the one we expect most people to keep on a counter. The sticks are the same serving
pre-measured in film, for travel, or for anyone who does not trust a level scoop at seven in the
morning.

The sticks cost a little more, and the reason is the film rather than the formula —
single-serve packaging is simply more expensive to make than a jar and a scoop. The difference
is on the shop page. The powder inside is identical, and we are not going to describe the more
expensive one as the better one.
