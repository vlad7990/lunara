Nothing ships for months. That leaves a single question about what goes on the internet in the
meantime, and the industry has a settled answer: a percentage off a thing that does not exist
yet, in exchange for an email address. We published the formula instead — every ingredient, at
its dose, with its unit, before the raw material was locked.

The reasoning is not sentimental. A discount gives you nothing to be curious about and nothing
to check. A formula gives you both.

## A blend exists so the split cannot be checked

United States label law lets a manufacturer group ingredients into a proprietary blend, print
one total for the group, and list the parts in descending order by weight. Both of those are
true statements about the label. Neither is the split.

A blend printed as "3,000 mg — myo-inositol, saffron extract" is perfectly consistent with
2,999 mg of the first and 1 mg of the second. Descending order tells you the rank. It never
tells you the magnitude. This is not a loophole anyone is hiding; it is on the front of a great
many labels, in plain sight, and it is how a product containing a trace of each can accurately
describe itself as built on clinically studied ingredients.

Once you have a blend, you have given up the ability to publish a dose card. That is not a side
effect of the decision. For most brands it is the decision.

## What the card costs us

Three things, and we would rather list them here than have you assume there is no cost.

It is copyable. Any competent formulator can read our card and file something close to it. We
decided we would rather be copied than be unreadable.

We cannot quietly reformulate. A dose that moves, moves in public, with a date on it.

And we are publishing targets. The card went up before raw-material selection finished, which
guarantees we are publicly wrong about something at least once before we ship. We already have
been. On 28 February myo-inositol was fixed at 3,000 mg, down from a 4,000 mg option, because
one scoop had to stay drinkable. That entry is in the change log, it is dated, and it stays
there after the number stops being news.

> A number you can check is worth more than an adjective you cannot.

## What the card has to survive

The card is not the marketing layer. It is the thing every other number on this site is checked
against: the Supplement Facts panel when the contract manufacturer produces it, the certificate
of analysis for each lot, and the code printed on the box that brings you back here to read it.

If those ever disagree with the card, the card is what is wrong, and saying so is the whole
point of having published it.

## The offer

It is narrower than a discount. You can read what is in this, at what dose, and why each thing
is there — including the things that are not in it, which have their own pieces in this series
— and decide from that whether any of it is worth your money.

That is the entire pitch. It is available to you now, months before anything is for sale, and it
does not require your email address.
